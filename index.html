Attendance & new hosts

What counts as attendance and how new hosts are assessed

Attendance standard: 80%

Hosts who work the whole period need 12 days per half-month and 24 days per month.

Hosts who join mid-period need 80% of their days on board (start day included), rounded to the nearest day. Example: start on the 10th, 6 days on board by the 15th, 5 days required.

Left: days on board. Right, in bold: days required.

New host assessment

StageTo passIf not passed
Trial (first 7 days)2K/day average, attendance not checked20% commission
New host (first 15 days, trial included)3K/day average and 80% attendanceAll diamonds from those 15 days at 20% commission

Passing the trial only starts the new host period. If both 15-day targets are met, all 15 days (trial included) are paid at ₱923/day, 15 days at most. From day 16 the regular host standard applies.

By a new host's first payday, the first 15 days are always complete (pay for the 1st–15th comes at month end, and for the 16th–month end on the 15th of the next month). So it is settled on the full 15-day average and attendance, with nothing to top up later.

Base pay, raises & bonuses

Daily rates, caps and when raises kick in

Base pay cap: 26 days a month

Once attendance is met, each half-month pays every day streamed. The full month pays at most 26 days, so the cap is the monthly base = daily rate × 26 days. From 300K in the month, the raise ladder monthly base is the cap.

Full-month back pay

If a host missed the daily rate in either half-month but catches up over the full month, the whole month is paid at the daily rate. It does not matter which half was missed.

Commission & paydays

How the 20% works and when money arrives

How the 20% commission works

20% commission = diamonds ÷ 100 × 50% × 20% × the USD to PHP rate. The calculator on this page uses a fixed rate of ₱59 as an estimate. It is for reference only, and the amount actually credited in the backend prevails.

PartHow it is paid
5% self-withdrawalGoes straight to your TikTok balance after each stream
15% paid by the companyPaid on payday
  • Base pay and commission are either-or: with base pay the company's 15% is not paid, and the 5% TikTok withdrawal is still yours.
  • When attendance is not met, the company-paid part is capped at ₱10,000 per half-month.
  • Any later back pay is reduced by commission the company already paid.

LIVE rules

Start on time, stream the full LIVE, overtime and the code of conduct

Deductions: late start, early end, split streams

Each LIVE is 6 hours at a fixed time: start on time and stream the full 6 hours. Each minute is ₱3.

SituationWhat happens
Late: up to 10 minutes after the start timeNot counted as late, no deduction
Late: more than 10 minutes after the start timeOnly the minutes beyond 10 are deducted, at ₱3 each. Example: 15 minutes late, 5 × ₱3 = ₱15
Early end: up to 30 minutes shortEvery minute short is deducted at ₱3, with no grace period. Example: 5 h 45 min streamed, 15 × ₱3 = ₱45
Early end: more than 30 minutes shortCounted as an invalid LIVE day, not counted toward attendance
Split stream: streaming in two partsCounted as an invalid LIVE day, not counted toward attendance, unless the team specially approved it. Example: 4 h morning + 2 h evening, or 3 h + 3 h
  • At payroll, split streams are first counted as invalid LIVE days. If the team approved yours, bring the approval to the team to have it settled again.
  • A stream cut off by an unexpected event also counts as a split stream. Keep your proof and apply through the appeal process.

Overtime (OT)

To work overtime, first ask the Host department for OT permission. Once approved, streaming a full 3 hours beyond the 6-hour LIVE counts as one OT and pays an extra ₱500.

SituationWhat happens
Approved, 3 full extra hoursCounts as OT, extra ₱500
Approved, under 3 extra hoursNot OT, no extra pay
Not requested or not approvedExtra time does not count as OT

OT is limited to 3 hours a day, so at most ₱500 OT pay a day.

Appeals

What to do if something special happens

Grounds for appeal

A host with fewer than 12 days can appeal to their team's appeal contact on any of these grounds:

  1. Illness that needs a long leave to rest
  2. A family emergency that needs a long leave
  3. Not in good condition and asking for rest to recover
  4. Repeated unexpected events (emergencies, sudden incidents)
  5. Other situations that need a specific explanation

Limit: all 5 grounds together allow one appeal every three months. They cannot be combined, and further requests are not approved.
